# ADR-014 — A 403 is usually "slow down", and honesty out-fetches impersonation

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Supersedes:** the "a 403 on `robots.txt` stays `search_snippet`" rule in
[ADR-012](ADR-012-robots-facts-and-feeds.md). That ADR's other decisions — measure robots
claims, prefer feeds to pages, a client-rendered page is not a source — all stand, as does
its ban on user-agent impersonation, which turns out to be even better founded than when
it was written.

## Context

ADR-012 was written one day after the chamber directory was written off as blocking us,
and it enshrined that conclusion: a 403 on `robots.txt` means the server refuses our
client, so the entry stays `search_snippet` forever and retrying with a browser
user-agent is forbidden. Jesse asked for the chamber to be forced anyway, which meant
actually measuring what the server does instead of reasoning from one failed request.

Every part of the original conclusion was wrong.

| Request | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `BlockPartyBot/1.0` (our honest UA) | **HTTP 200**, 432 KB |
| A full Chrome user-agent string | **HTTP 403** |

The site was never blocking us. It blocks clients that *pretend to be browsers* — an
ordinary anti-scraper rule. Impersonating Chrome is not only against the rules here, it
is the one approach that reliably fails.

The 403s on `robots.txt` were our own doing twice over. `RobotFileParser.read()` fetches
with urllib's default user-agent rather than ours, and Python turns any 401/403 on
`robots.txt` into `disallow_all`. And the file we eventually did read says:

```
# hestiacp autogenerated robots.txt
User-agent: *
Crawl-delay: 10
```

No `Disallow` at all. It permits everyone and asks one thing — ten seconds between
requests — which the fetcher was not doing. The 403s were the site asking us to slow
down, and we recorded them as a refusal to be crawled.

## Decision

**`robots.txt` is fetched with our own user-agent**, not the HTTP library's default. A
site that has allowlisted our bot, or that rejects unknown clients, must be asked the
question as us.

**RFC 9309 governs an unreadable `robots.txt`.** A 4xx means no robots.txt exists and the
site may be crawled; a 5xx or a network failure means unknown, and we stay out. Python's
stricter reading of 401/403 is not followed, because it silently converts a throttle into
a permanent exclusion.

**`Crawl-delay` is obeyed.** It is the only thing most robots.txt files ask for, and
ignoring it is what produced every 403 in this story. Reading `robots.txt` counts as a
request for delay purposes — without that, the first real fetch lands milliseconds later
and is refused.

**Where robots.txt cannot be read, the delay defaults to 10 seconds** rather than zero. A
host that will not serve its own robots.txt is usually throttling, not welcoming.

**A 403 from a crawl-delayed host is retried with backoff before a source is called
blocked.** A source demoted to manual work fails silently — nothing errors, it just
quietly costs a human every month. That asymmetry justifies the retries.

**Impersonating a browser remains forbidden**, and this is the reinforcement: it does not
work, and a site that filters browser-shaped strings is stating a preference as clearly
as a robots rule. We identify ourselves and take what we are given.

## Consequences

- The chamber directory is fetchable and is a normal source again. Its ~36 members were
  out of reach for two days over a misreading.
- Access and readability are different problems and both have to be solved. Getting past
  the 403 still left a page whose member list is rendered client-side; the members live
  on the 22 category pages, which the collector now walks at the stated pace. That is
  roughly four minutes, which is fine for a monthly job and not fine for a daily one.
- Every `search_snippet` entry justified by a 403 is now suspect. `kennedy.isd742.org`
  and `csbsju.edu` carry real `Disallow` rules and are unaffected.
- Obeying `Crawl-delay` makes runs slower and occasionally much slower. That is correct.
  A registry that scrapes a neighbour's small business at full speed is not the good
  neighbour this app claims to be.
- Two ADRs in two days have now corrected facts that were written down confidently and
  never measured. The lesson is not about robots.txt: a claim about the outside world
  that nobody has tested does not belong in an accepted ADR.
