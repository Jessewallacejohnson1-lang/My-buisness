# Hygge — Founder Context & Decision Log

## The bet
What I believe that others don't: Our surroundings dictate how we feel and act. Community momentum compounds — it's never neutral, always moving positive or negative. Most wellness apps ignore place entirely.
Why this works if it works: Make it as frictionless as possible to attend local events and connect with community. When showing up is easy, momentum builds.
What kills it: People find the app hard to use or useless. Retention through usefulness is the core risk — not acquisition.

## Locked decisions (and why)
- Hyperlocal, one town at a time — because place shapes behavior; you can't manufacture community at scale from the start
- Joe Town first — because Jesse grew up there, has real relationships with business owners, knows the social fabric; 7,000 population is manageable to actually saturate
- Subscription model — $2/month per person; low friction to try, recurring revenue
- Hand-drawn / earthy / time-of-day brand — aesthetic direction, not load-bearing yet; revisit once core functionality is solid

## Ruled out (don't reopen without new info)
- Chasing features/polish before usefulness — the app lives or dies on whether people open it daily, not on how it looks

## Expansion roadmap
1. St. Joseph (pop. 7,000) — prove the model, target 1,000 monthly users
2. St. Cloud metro (pop. 100,000+) — a few thousand users makes this profitable
3. Other cities — repeat the playbook

## Revenue philosophy (current stage)
Not optimizing for profit yet — optimizing for scale and retention. $2/month is intentionally low friction. First profits go into hosting free local events for app members — events are marketing, not cost centers.

## Founder context
Jesse is in high school with 2+ years of runway before any external pressure. No investor clock. Can afford to scale slow and right.

## Success metrics
- St. Joe: 1,000 monthly users (best case)
- St. Cloud: a few thousand monthly users = first real profit
- Early signal (60-day): 100 DAU in St. Joe

## Open questions I'm wrestling with
- What specifically makes the app useful enough to open daily — events alone, or does the health/nutrition loop pull its weight?
- What does the St. Cloud launch playbook look like — does it require the same founder-relationship advantage, or does the model work without it?

## What I actually know vs. assume
Know (from real people/data): St. Joseph has positive community momentum; Jesse has real relationships with local business owners; community already exists and is active
Assuming (untested): $2/month is a price people will pay; frictionless event attendance is the core value prop that drives retention
Know: Jesse has personal networks in St. Joseph, St. Cloud, and the Twin Cities — warm distribution in all three markets, not just the first
